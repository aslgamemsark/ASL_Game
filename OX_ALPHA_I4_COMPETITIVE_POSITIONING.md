## 1. The landscape (from public comparisons)

| Competitor | Model | Camera feedback | Price | Platform |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lingvano** | Subscription course | "Sign Mirror" — real-time visual check | Paid sub (free trial) | iOS/Android/Web |
| **ASL Bloom** | Subscription course | No real-time signing check | Freemium | iOS/Android |
| **The ASL App** | Dictionary/life-skills pack | None | Free + IAP | iOS/Android |
| **InterSign ASL** | Course + games | Limited | Freemium | Multi |
| **SignSchool** | Free lessons/dictionary | None real-time | Free | Web + app |
| **Preply/tutors** | Human tutors | Human feedback | $$/hr | Live video |

Common thread across every roundup: **real-time camera feedback is the rarest feature** — only Lingvano
ships it broadly, behind a subscription and native apps.

## 2. QuickSign's actual differentiators (each verified by an executed probe)

1. **Real-time, per-parameter feedback, free, no account, in the browser.** The exact combination none
   of the free competitors offer: G2 measured a guest reaching the live verifier in 6.9 s with zero
   signup; ParameterChecklist names handshape/orientation/location/movement (I1 verified the claim is
   TRUE).
2. **Privacy as architecture, not policy.** Recognition runs fully on-device; "camera never leaves your
   device" survived the I1 honesty audit because it's literally true (D3). Lingvano's camera features
   have no equivalent public privacy guarantee.
3. **Deaf-first interaction design.** F3 found zero hearing-norm shaming copy; E4/E2/E3 verified
   keyboard/SR/muted operability. No competitor rounds up public evidence for this.
4. **Honest failure states.** D3's denied/offline cards, F2's no-shaming encourage bank, H4's economy
   that refuses to reward skipping.

## 3. Honest gaps (where competitors win today)

- **Content depth**: Lingvano/Bloom ship hundreds of lessons across grammar/conversation; H6 counts
  ~1 hour of novel QuickSign content (16 lessons, 5 stories, 51 signs). This is THE competitive gap.
- **Native distribution**: they're in the stores; QuickSign's TWA path is prepared but unpackaged (I3:
  assetlinks live, AAB = owner scope).
- **Grammar/conversation**: competitors teach sentence-level ASL; QuickSign teaches vocabulary +
  fingerspelling scenarios.
- **Brand trust**: they have reviews and years of presence; QuickSign is unknown.

## 4. Positioning statement (evidence-backed)

> **QuickSign is the only free, no-signup way to practice ASL signs with instant per-parameter feedback
> in your browser — your camera never leaves your device.**

Every clause of that sentence has a committed probe or report behind it (G2/I1/D3/H6 respectively).
It is defensible precisely because it claims less than the subscription giants do, and delivers exactly
what it claims.

## 5. Strategic read for the owner

- The wedge is **"try before you commit to a subscription"**: QuickSign costs nothing and asks nothing;
  its natural funnel feeds learners toward deeper (possibly paid/other) study after the first hour.
- Closing the content gap (H6's unit list: numbers/colors/family/feelings) does more for positioning
  than any feature work — it converts "impressive demo" into "credible first month".
- Deaf-community credibility (F3's contract) is a moat the big apps don't advertise; keeping that
  standard while scaling content is the strategic constraint worth protecting.

**Verdict:** positioning is coherent and honest today; the single highest-leverage investment remains
content depth, not differentiation work — the differentiator already exists and is proven.
