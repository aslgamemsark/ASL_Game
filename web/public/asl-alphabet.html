The ASL alphabet: all 26 letters, explained

Fingerspelling is usually the first thing new signers learn — one handshape per letter of the English alphabet, used for names, places, and words that don't have their own sign yet. Why learn the ASL alphabet first?

Three reasons fingerspelling is almost always where beginners start.

1

It's finite

26 handshapes, no grammar or sentence structure to learn yet — a manageable first goal before tackling full ASL vocabulary.

2

You'll use it immediately

Names, places, and brand names are almost always fingerspelled — you'll need it in your very first real conversation.

3

It trains your hands

Fine motor control and handshape precision built here carry directly into learning full signs afterward.

The full ASL alphabet, A–Z

Every letter is a static handshape (no movement) held in front of your body, palm generally facing out — except J and Z, which trace a small shape in the air.

A

Letter A

Fist with thumb resting at the side.

B

Letter B

Flat hand, fingers together, thumb tucked across the palm.

C

Letter C

Fingers and thumb curved into the outline of a C.

D

Letter D

Index finger up, other fingers curl in to touch the thumb.

E

Letter E

All four fingers bent at the middle knuckle, thumb tucked underneath.

F

Letter F

Thumb and index finger touch in a circle; other three fingers stand up.

G

Letter G

Index and thumb extended, hand turned sideways so the index points across.

H

Letter H

Index and middle fingers extended together, hand turned sideways.

I

Letter I

Fist with only the pinky finger raised.

J

Letter J

Pinky extended, trace a J shape in the air — down, then a hook to the left.

K

Letter K

Index and middle fingers spread apart, thumb touching the middle finger's base.

L

Letter L

Index finger up, thumb out to the side — a clear "L" shape.

M

Letter M

Index, middle, and ring fingers curled down over the tucked thumb.

N

Letter N

Closed fist, thumb tucked under just the index and middle fingers.

O

Letter O

All fingertips curl in to meet the thumb, forming a rounded O.

P

Letter P

Same handshape as K, hand turned to point downward.

Q

Letter Q

Same handshape as G, hand turned to point downward.

R

Letter R

Index and middle fingers extended and crossed.

S

Letter S

Fist with the thumb folded across the front of the fingers.

T

Letter T

Closed fist with the thumb tucked between the index and middle fingers.

U

Letter U

Index and middle fingers extended together, pointing straight up.

V

Letter V

Index and middle fingers up and apart — the classic peace sign.

W

Letter W

Index, middle, and ring fingers up; thumb and pinky curled in.

X

Letter X

Index finger bent into a hook shape, other fingers curled into the palm.

Y

Letter Y

Thumb and pinky extended out, other three fingers curled — "hang loose."

Z

Letter Z

Index finger extended, trace a Z zigzag in the air.

Frequently asked questions

How many letters are in the ASL alphabet?

26 — one handshape per letter of the English alphabet, all signed one-handed (traditionally the dominant hand).

What is fingerspelling used for?

Fingerspelling the ASL alphabet is used for proper nouns (names, places, brands) and words that don't have an established sign yet — it's a bridge, not a replacement for ASL's own vocabulary and grammar.

Is the ASL alphabet the same as British Sign Language?

No. BSL fingerspelling is two-handed and uses a completely different set of handshapes from the one-handed ASL alphabet shown on this page.
